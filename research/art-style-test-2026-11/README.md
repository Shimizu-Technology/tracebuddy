# TraceBuddy 2.0 — Art Style Test (Nov 2026)

This test evaluates three candidate illustration styles across six subjects (`happy-crab`, `island-turtle`, `dream-unicorn`, `rocket-ship`, `latte-stone`, `sleepy-cat`) next to the current hand-coded 1.x SVGs in `shared/drawings.ts`, plus an `Easy` vs `More detail` test on Happy Crab in S1.

All 20 line-art drawings were thresholded with `magick`, vectorized with `potrace`, normalized to `viewBox="0 0 1000 1000"` with an 8% margin (`vector/`), and round-trip verified with `rsvg-convert`. Color previews (`raw/*-color.png`) were generated by image-to-image colorization directly on top of each approved line-art drawing so outlines match 1:1. See [prompts.md](./prompts.md) for verbatim style prompts, tool/model notes, commercial terms caveats, and the rejection log.

## Style Assessments

### S1 — Storybook Soft (ages 2–6)
Thick, uniform rounded outlines and plump proportions make S1 immediately traceable for young children—both with a finger on glass and through paper over a backlit screen where thin lines disappear. Every shape stays open and forgiving, with generous white gaps between limbs and shell panels. Charm is warm and approachable without feeling generic, and consistency across all six subjects held tight under a single verbatim prompt. Vectorization was spotless: SVGs came out clean and lightweight (11–28 KB, 1–6 paths per base subject) with zero blobs or broken contours, and `crab-levels.png` shows the style flexes cleanly from toddler `Easy` (4 legs, eyes directly on shell) to `More detail` (jointed limbs, spots, bubbles) without breaking character. The main risk is that very minimal monuments like the Latte Stone can look plain unless `More detail` adds thoughtful bold accents, and older kids (8+) or adults browsing built-in templates may find the proportions slightly young (though adults primarily trace via My Photo).

### S2 — Picture-Book Ink (ages 4–10 & adults)
S2 has the highest illustration charm in the set—especially on the Guam Latte Stone (carved limestone contours and a five-petal plumeria), the curled Sleepy Cat, and the vintage Rocket. The subtle pen-weight variation looks like a printed children's book rather than an app icon pack, and consistency across the six drawings stayed strong. Surprisingly, `potrace` preserved even fine details like rocket rivets, mane strands, and grass blades cleanly at 2000×2000, though the resulting SVGs are roughly twice as heavy (34–52 KB, up to 53 paths). The real risk is traceability and production QA for young kids: ages 2–5 will struggle with tight clusters (crab claw segments, rocket rivets, unicorn mane layers, turtle flipper spots), thin interior lines fade quickly when paper-tracing at low opacity, and scaling to 40–60 subjects with Easy/More detail pairs will take stricter manual QA to keep sketchy lines and cramped spaces out.

### S3 — Modern Geometric (premium kids' app branding)
S3 delivers the boldest thumbnail silhouette on pastel tiles (`tile-preview.png`) and the leanest vectors in the test (9–27 KB, 1–5 paths). Crisp circles, semicircles, and capsules give toddlers clear, predictable shapes to trace and keep stroke weight dead even across subjects. Consistency is high, and the Latte Stone and Rocket look sharp and confident. The tradeoff is warmth and range: organic animals stiffen up noticeably—the top-down Sea Turtle has a rigid geometric stare and the Unicorn feels like a wooden toy rather than a living character. Stretching strict circles and capsules across 40–60 drawings—especially organic Guam subjects like the ko'ko' bird, carabao, coconut crab, and bougainvillea—carries a real risk of sliding back toward the flat icon look 2.0 is trying to replace.

---

## Recommendation: **S1 — Storybook Soft**

Pick **S1 ("Storybook soft")** for TraceBuddy 2.0's built-in library. TraceBuddy's spec puts pre-readers (ages 2–8) first on the Home grid while serving older kids and adults through My Photo and `More detail`. S1 is the only style that hits every hard requirement at once: thick even outlines that survive real paper tracing, instant warmth on the 2.0 pastel tiles, small reliable `potrace` vectors, high cross-subject consistency, and a proven `Easy` / `More detail` progression (`crab-levels.png`). For the signature Guam set, keep S1's bold rounded line weight so the library stays one family, and borrow S2's restrained cultural touches (like a bold five-petal plumeria or subtle stone contour on `More detail`) rather than switching the whole app to thinner ink lines.

## Subjects That Were Hard

- **Latte Stone (`latte-stone`) was by far the hardest subject for accuracy.** On the first pass for both S1 and S2, the generator added an overhanging rectangular rim bar across the top of the `tasa` capstone (and a flared collar on the S1 `haligi` pillar), turning the monument into something resembling a birdbath or flowerpot. Explicitly forbidding rims, collars, coffee mugs, and cartoon faces—and describing the `tasa` as a smooth rimless semicircle/D-shape flat-side-up—solved S1 on a clean regen and fixed S2 via targeted image edit. Expect to use strict shape constraints (and visual reference sheets once locked) for every culturally specific Guam subject (`ko'ko'`, `proa`, `carabao`, `coconut crab`, `bougainvillea`) and review each one with Leon before shipping.
- **Framing margins:** On wide horizontal subjects (crabs) and tall vertical ones (rockets), the raw generator often settled around 6–11% white margins rather than the requested 14%. None were cropped, and the `magick` + `potrace` normalization step cleanly enforces an exact 8% margin on every vector SVG.
- **No-text / anatomy guardrails:** Explicitly specifying leg counts on crabs (6 walking legs + 2 claws; 4 stubby legs on Easy) and forbidding floating `"Zzz"` letters on Sleepy Cat prevented common AI line-art failures on the first try.
